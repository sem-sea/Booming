Key takeaways

In a June 2026 Ponemon Institute and NOLA study of 320 enterprise security decision-makers, 51% used chatbots for product research. Security vendors get named in AI answers when peers, analysts and research reports name them first, and when their own pages answer technical questions in plain text.

Security buyers research differently from most B2B buyers. They are skeptical of marketing, check claims and lean on peers. The Ponemon study found peer recommendations the top source for vendor discovery at 55%, and research reports the strongest influence on final selection at 46% (Ponemon and NOLA).

AI now sits in the middle of that process: 51% of respondents use chatbots and 48% use AI overviews for product research. Microsoft Copilot deserves special attention, because Forrester found business buyers four times as likely as consumers to use it.

And when buyers ask for a shortlist, assistants lean on lists. In an Ahrefs study of 750 ChatGPT prompts, "best X" lists made up 43.8% of the page types cited.

What is AI visibility for a cybersecurity vendor?

AI visibility for a cybersecurity vendor is how often assistants like ChatGPT and Copilot name your product when security buyers ask for options.

It has two parts. Being named in shortlist answers, such as "best XDR for mid-size banks". And being described correctly when a buyer asks about you directly: certifications, deployment model, integrations and pricing approach.

Why do security buyers trust some AI answers more than others?

Trust in an AI answer means the buyer can check it: security leaders still weigh peers, research reports and technical depth above any summary.

The same study found that 52% of security leaders say vendor marketing lacks technical depth, and 49% miss ROI justification. An AI answer built from vague product pages inherits that problem. An answer that cites your architecture page, your test results or an independent report gives the buyer something to verify.

Which prompts should a security vendor track?

A security prompt set is a list of buyer questions by category, compliance need and company size, such as "best XDR for mid-size banks".

How do cybersecurity vendors get recommended?

Getting recommended means being named in the sources assistants read: analyst notes, peer reviews, best-of lists, research reports and your own technical pages.

  1. Map 50 to 100 prompts across your categories, regulations and target sectors, in every language your buyers use.
  2. Publish technical answer pages. Architecture, integrations, deployment time and data residency, in HTML text and tables, not only in PDFs.
  3. Build a trust center. SOC 2, ISO 27001 and other certifications as text, with dates and scope.
  4. Publish original research. Research reports had the strongest direct effect on selection in the Ponemon study. Put the key findings on an open page so assistants can cite them; gate the full report if you must.
  5. Grow peer reviews on G2, Gartner Peer Insights and PeerSpot. Peers are the top discovery source.
  6. Get onto independent best-of lists for your category. Our guide on getting onto the lists AI assistants cite covers how.

Frequently asked questions

Does Microsoft Copilot matter for security vendors?

Yes. Many enterprise security teams work inside Microsoft 365, and Forrester found business buyers four times as likely as consumers to use Copilot. Copilot searches Bing, so Bing indexing matters.

Should we gate our research reports?

Gate the full PDF if leads depend on it, but publish the key findings, charts and method on an open HTML page. Assistants cannot cite what they cannot read.

Do review platforms help AI visibility?

They are among the sources assistants read for shortlist questions, and peers were the top discovery source for security buyers in the Ponemon study. A steady flow of recent reviews helps both.

How do we know what AI says about us?

Track a fixed prompt set monthly in a tool such as Otterly.ai or Peec AI, and check the AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools for Copilot citations.

Can AI answers get our product wrong?

Yes, especially on certifications and integrations. Publish those facts in plain text on your own site and correct outdated third-party pages where you can.
