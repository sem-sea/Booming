What is an AI visibility metric?

An AI visibility metric is a repeatable number that shows how AI assistants present your brand, measured on a fixed prompt set each month.

Repeatable is the key word. AI answers vary between runs and users, so a single screenshot proves nothing. A fixed set of prompts, run on a schedule, gives you a trend.

Which five metrics belong in a board report?

A board-ready AI metric is one that links to revenue or risk: share of voice, citation share, answer accuracy, branded demand and AI-sourced pipeline.

How do you connect AI visibility to pipeline?

Connecting AI visibility to pipeline means combining a self-reported source field, CRM opportunity data and branded demand, since most AI answers send no click.

Add "How did you hear about us?" to every form and sales intake, with ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot and Gemini as options. Tag those opportunities in the CRM. What should a CMO not report?

A vanity AI metric is a number that moves without affecting buyers, such as mentions on irrelevant prompts or a vendor score without raw answers.

  • Mention counts on prompts no buyer asks.
  • A single visibility score from a tool, without the answers behind it.
  • Screenshots of one good answer.
  • Traffic from AI referrals alone; most AI influence never clicks.

Frequently asked questions

How often should we report AI visibility?

Monthly to the marketing team, quarterly to the board. Monthly data shows trends; quarterly reporting smooths out the noise of varying answers.

How many prompts do we need?

Usually 50 to 150 per market, covering problem questions, solution comparisons and shortlist questions in every language your buyers use.

How do we handle answers that change between runs?

Run each prompt several times per period and report the share of runs where you appear. Trends over months matter more than any single run.

Can we benchmark against competitors?

Yes. Track the same prompts for three main competitors and report share of voice as a percentage of all mentions.

What is a good share of voice?

There is no universal benchmark. Compare against your direct competitors and track the direction over time.
