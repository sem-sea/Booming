Key takeaways

In an Ahrefs study of 750 ChatGPT prompts, "best X" lists made up 43.8% of cited page types. Getting onto independent lists that are updated often is one of the most direct ways to show up when buyers ask AI for a shortlist.

When a buyer asks ChatGPT for the best tools or agencies in a category, the answer often borrows from existing rankings. Ahrefs found that 79.1% of 1,100 cited blog lists had been updated in 2025, and 26% in the two months before the study (Ahrefs). Fresh lists win.

Authority matters less than you might expect: 35% of cited lists sat on low-authority domains. But lists are not a sure bet. Seer Interactive measured a drop in ChatGPT citations of listicle URLs from 160,000 to 111,000 between December 2025 and January 2026, about 30%.

So lists are one channel among several. Buyers use them anyway: Forrester reports that 94% of business buyers use AI in their buying process.

What is a best-of list in AI search?

A best-of list in AI search is a ranked comparison page, like "best CRM tools", that assistants read and summarize when buyers ask for options.

Lists come in several forms, and you control them to different degrees.

Why do AI assistants rely on these lists?

List reliance means assistants borrow a shortlist someone already made: a ranked page answers "which ones?" faster than ten separate vendor sites.

A shortlist question has no single right answer, so assistants look for pages that already compare options. Find the lists they cite for your prompts, and you know where you need to be.

How do you get onto the lists that matter?

Getting listed means finding the pages assistants already cite for your category, then giving their authors a reason and the facts to include you.

  1. Run your shortlist prompts in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot, and write down every list they cite.
  2. Sort the lists by how often they appear, how fresh they are and how well they fit your segment.
  3. Check what each list needs: pricing, a use case, screenshots, customer proof. Most authors list what they compare.
  4. Prepare a one-page fact sheet with exactly those facts, so including you takes the author ten minutes.
  5. Pitch with a reason: a segment the list misses, a new feature, or a customer result the author can quote.
  6. Grow independent reviews, so review-ranked category pages move without a pitch.
  7. Follow up when lists refresh. Many authors update yearly; be in their inbox before that.

Should you publish your own best-of list?

A self-published list is a comparison on your own site that ranks you; it can get cited when it is fair, sourced and current.

Many companies do it. Of 250 "best X software" results pages Ahrefs checked in Google, 169 (67.6%) featured a list where the publisher ranked itself first. A first-party list also appeared in 34% of ChatGPT software answers that recommended the publisher.

If you publish one, keep it honest: real criteria, competitors described fairly, a date, and sources. A list that reads as an advert helps nobody, and buyers notice.

Frequently asked questions

Can we pay to get onto lists?

Some lists sell placements or earn affiliate fees. Disclosed sponsorship is legal, but buying links that pass ranking value breaks Google's spam policies. Earned placements on independent lists are the safer route.

How many lists should we target?

Start with the five to ten lists that appear most often in your prompt results. Add more when new ones show up in your monthly tracking.

Are listicles losing value in AI search?

Seer measured a 30% drop in ChatGPT listicle citations in one month. Treat lists as one channel next to reviews, media mentions and your own answer pages.

Do Dutch-language lists matter?

They can. Assistants usually answer in the language of the question, and the sources they cite can differ by language. Track your Dutch prompts separately.

How do we know if a list placement worked?

Rerun the same prompts monthly and track whether you are named and which sources are cited. Tools such as Otterly.ai or Peec AI automate this.
