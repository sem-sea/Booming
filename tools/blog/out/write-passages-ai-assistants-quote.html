Key takeaways

In the GEO study from Princeton and IIT Delhi, adding quotations lifted a source's visibility in generative answers by about 41%, adding statistics by about 31% and citing sources by about 28%. Keyword stuffing lowered it. Write specific, sourced passages that answer one question each.

The researchers built GEO-bench, a set of 10,000 queries, and tested how content changes affected a source's visibility in generative engine answers. The study was accepted at KDD 2024 (arXiv).

Measured by position-adjusted word count, quotation addition scored about 41% above the unoptimized baseline, statistics addition about 31%, citing sources about 28% and fluency optimization about 28%. Keyword stuffing scored about 8% below baseline (Aggarwal et al.).

Assistants quote a lot of sources: ChatGPT cites sources in 96% of its responses, about five per answer (Muck Rack, May 2026). Each one is a passage someone wrote.

What makes a passage quotable for AI?

A quotable passage is a short, self-contained block that answers one question directly, with a specific fact, number or attributed quote.

Assistants work with passages, not whole pages. A paragraph that only makes sense after reading three others is hard to reuse.

Which writing techniques had the biggest effect?

The most effective GEO technique is adding quotations, followed by statistics and cited sources, while keyword stuffing had a negative effect in the study.

How do you write passages AI assistants quote?

Writing quotable passages means one question per section, the answer first, then a sourced number or quote, in plain sentences under 25 words.

  1. Use a question as the heading, phrased the way buyers ask.
  2. Answer in the first two sentences, so the passage stands alone.
  3. Add one specific number with its source and year.
  4. Add a quote from a named expert, customer or document where it helps.
  5. Link the original source, not a summary of it.
  6. Cut filler and repetition; keyword stuffing lowered visibility in the study.

Frequently asked questions

Is GEO writing different from good writing?

Mostly not. The techniques that worked, quotes, numbers and sources, are what good analysts already do.

How long should a passage be?

Short enough to stand alone: a heading, a two-sentence answer and one supporting fact.

Do the results apply to ChatGPT and Gemini?

The study tested generative engines in a research setting. The direction fits what citation studies show, but exact gains will differ.

Should we add quotes to every section?

Only where a real quote adds evidence. Invented or generic quotes damage trust.

Does keyword stuffing still hurt?

It scored below baseline in the GEO study, and Google's spam policies prohibit it.
