Key takeaways

AI assistants get facts wrong often: a Tow Center test found eight AI search tools answered more than 60% of queries incorrectly, and an EBU and BBC study found significant issues in 45% of answers. Check what assistants say about your company monthly and fix the sources they rely on.

Wrong answers are not rare edge cases. The Tow Center for Digital Journalism ran 1,600 queries across eight AI search tools in early 2025; collectively, they answered more than 60% incorrectly (Columbia Journalism Review).

A larger study by 22 public service broadcasters reviewed more than 3,000 answers from ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity in 14 languages. 45% had at least one significant issue, and 31% had serious sourcing problems (EBU).

Marketing leaders are responding. Gartner found in November 2026 that 42% of CMOs monitor and correct misinformation about their brand in AI output.

What counts as a wrong AI answer about your company?

A wrong AI answer is any statement about your company that misstates facts buyers use to decide: pricing, features, certifications, customers, locations or ownership.

The common ones in B2B: outdated pricing, a discontinued product presented as current, missing certifications, a competitor's feature attributed to you, or the wrong headquarters country.

Why do AI assistants get companies wrong?

An AI error about a company usually means the assistant relied on outdated, thin or conflicting sources, or filled a gap where no source existed.

If your pricing page hides prices, a review site from 2023 becomes the source. If your certifications sit in a PDF, the assistant may not find them. Every gap is an invitation to guess.

How do you correct wrong AI answers?

Correcting AI answers means fixing the sources, not the assistant: publish the right facts clearly, update outdated third-party pages and re-check monthly.

  1. Run a fact check monthly. Ask ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity ten questions buyers ask about you.
  2. Log each error with the assistant, the wrong statement and the cited source.
  3. Publish the correct fact on your own site in plain HTML text: a pricing page, a trust page, an about page.
  4. Fix third-party sources: update review profiles, directories, partner pages and outdated articles where you can.
  5. Use the feedback buttons in each assistant to report the wrong answer.
  6. Re-check after four weeks and track the error rate over time.

Frequently asked questions

Can we ask OpenAI or Google to correct an answer?

You can report answers with the feedback options in each product. The lasting fix is correcting the sources assistants rely on.

Which facts matter most?

The ones that end deals: pricing model, certifications, integrations, customer segments and whether a product still exists.

How often should we check?

Monthly for core facts, and after every launch, price change or rebrand.

Do buyers notice wrong AI answers?

Many do. TrustRadius found that 94% of buyers who use AI fact-check its answers at least some of the time.

Who should own this?

Marketing usually owns it, with input from product and sales, who hear the wrong claims first.
