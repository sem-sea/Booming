Key takeaways

Company sites do get cited, but not the pages most teams invest in: homepages and landing pages made up 23.8% of ChatGPT's top 1,000 cited pages, educational pages 19.4% and blog articles 1.9% (Ahrefs, 2025). Make your core pages the clearest answer about you.

Ahrefs analyzed the 1,000 pages ChatGPT cited most in September 2025. Wikipedia led with 29.7%, followed by homepages and landing pages at 23.8% and educational pages at 19.4%. Blog articles made up 1.9% and corporate pages 0.5% (Ahrefs).

Across industries, company content matters more. Muck Rack found corporate content averaged 24% of AI citations, rising to 36.6% in manufacturing (Muck Rack, May 2026).

In B2B categories, company websites made up 18.7% of citations in a 9.5 million citation study, next to user-generated platforms at 47.5% and peer review sites at 15% (Meltwater).

Which company pages get cited most?

Company page citation is led by homepages and landing pages, followed by educational pages that define or explain something clearly.

Why do so few blog posts get cited?

The likely blog problem is repetition: many posts restate what other pages already say, so assistants prefer the original definition, product page or primary data.

Ranking is not required either: 28.3% of the top cited pages had no organic keywords at all in the Ahrefs data. Being the clearest source counts more.

How do you make your core pages citable?

Making core pages citable means stating facts plainly: what you do, for whom, at what price, with which integrations and certifications, in HTML text.

  1. Rewrite your homepage opening in one plain sentence: what you do and for whom.
  2. Give every product its own page with features, integrations and pricing approach.
  3. Turn documentation into public HTML, not PDFs behind a login.
  4. Add a glossary or explainer section for the terms your buyers search.
  5. Put company facts on the about page: founding year, locations, team, customers.

Frequently asked questions

Should we stop blogging?

No, but write posts that add something new: data, experience or a clear opinion. Rewrites of existing content rarely get cited.

Why is Wikipedia so dominant?

It made up 29.7% of ChatGPT's top citations. For most B2B companies, a Wikipedia article is not realistic; focus on your own pages and reviews.

Do documentation pages count?

Educational pages, which include explainers and documentation, made up 19.4% of top citations. Public docs help.

Does our homepage need to rank to be cited?

Not necessarily. 28.3% of the top cited pages had no organic keywords in the Ahrefs study.

What should the about page include?

Hard facts: who you are, where you operate, what you sell, key customers and leadership.
