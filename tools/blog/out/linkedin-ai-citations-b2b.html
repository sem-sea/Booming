Key takeaways

LinkedIn ranked second among cited domains in a Meltwater and LinkedIn study of 9.5 million AI citations, behind YouTube. About 75% of cited LinkedIn content came from individual members, mostly text posts, and 48% was less than three months old.

For B2B topics, LinkedIn is now a major AI source. The Meltwater study, published in May 2026, tracked citations in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot and Claude. LinkedIn ranked first in AI and data science and in marketing and advertising categories (Meltwater).

People beat pages: about 75% of LinkedIn citations came from individual profiles and 25% from company pages. Accounts with 1,000 to 10,000 followers contributed 40% of citations (Meltwater).

Buyers use what assistants find. Gartner reported in May 2026 that 45% of B2B buyers used generative AI in a recent purchase, mainly to research vendors and products.

What LinkedIn content gets cited by AI?

Cited LinkedIn content is mostly recent, original text posts from individual experts, often formatted as lists, comparisons or how-to-choose guides.

Why do AI assistants cite LinkedIn posts?

LinkedIn citation means assistants treat professionals' first-hand posts as evidence of how practitioners see a topic, especially in B2B categories.

Its share grew 26% over the four-week research period. Recency matters: content older than 12 months made up only 12% of citations.

What should B2B teams post on LinkedIn?

A LinkedIn plan for AI visibility means experts posting original, specific text regularly, using the formats assistants cite: comparisons, lists and decision guides.

  1. Post from people, not only the company page. Founders, practice leads and engineers.
  2. Write original text with a clear point and specific numbers.
  3. Use citable formats: "how to choose", "X vs Y", "five mistakes we see".
  4. Post consistently, since recent content dominates citations.
  5. Link to the deeper page on your site for the full answer.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need a big following?

No. In the Meltwater data, 51% of cited creators had fewer than 10,000 followers.

Do company pages get cited?

Yes, but less: about 25% of LinkedIn citations came from company pages.

Are LinkedIn articles better than posts?

Text posts made up 72% of cited content, articles 12%. Short, specific posts work.

How often should experts post?

Regularly enough to stay recent; 48% of cited content was under three months old.

Does LinkedIn replace our blog?

No. Posts get cited for perspective; your site holds the facts, pricing and depth.
