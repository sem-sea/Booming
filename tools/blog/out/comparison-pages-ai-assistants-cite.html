What is a comparison page?

A comparison page is a page that sets your product against alternatives on the same criteria, usually titled "X vs Y" or "X alternatives".

Done well, it is a buying tool: the criteria buyers care about, honest answers per option, and a clear statement of who each option suits.

Why do AI assistants cite comparison pages?

Comparison citation means the assistant reuses structured answers: a page with criteria, a table and verdicts answers "which is better for me?" in one place.

A table with the same rows for every option is easy to quote and summarize. That is the format assistants lean on when a buyer asks for a recommendation.

How do you write a comparison page that gets cited?

Writing a citable comparison means choosing real buyer criteria, filling a table honestly for every option, and saying clearly who should pick which.

  1. Pick the comparisons buyers actually make. Check sales calls, lost deals and your prompt tracker.
  2. Use the buyer's criteria: price model, integrations, deployment time, support, compliance.
  3. Build one table with the same rows for every option, in HTML text.
  4. Source competitor facts from their own pricing and documentation pages, with a date.
  5. Write a verdict per segment: "Pick X if you need...", including cases where the competitor fits better.
  6. Review every quarter, because prices and features change.

Frequently asked questions

Is it risky to name competitors?

Comparative claims must be accurate and verifiable. Link to their public pages, date your facts and avoid claims you cannot prove.

How many comparison pages should we publish?

Start with the three to five comparisons your buyers make most often. Quality matters more than volume.

Should we admit where a competitor is better?

Yes. Buyers notice one-sided pages, and an honest verdict makes the rest of the page credible.

Where should comparison pages live?

On your own site, linked from product and pricing pages, so buyers and crawlers find them.

Do comparison pages work for services, not just software?

Yes. Compare engagement models, such as agency vs in-house vs freelancer, on cost, speed and control.
