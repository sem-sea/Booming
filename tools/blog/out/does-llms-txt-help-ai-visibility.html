Key takeaways

No measurable effect so far: SE Ranking found no correlation between llms.txt and AI citations across 300,000 domains (October 2026), and Ahrefs data showed 97% of llms.txt files got zero requests in May 2026. Keep one if it costs nothing. Spend the budget elsewhere.

Few GEO tactics spread as fast as llms.txt, and few have this little evidence behind them. SE Ranking found that 10.13% of the domains it studied had adopted the file. When the researchers removed llms.txt from their prediction model for AI citations, the model got more accurate, not less (SE Ranking).

Ahrefs looked at the demand side. Of about 38,000 domains with a valid file, only about 1,100 received any request in May 2026. AI retrieval bots linked to ChatGPT and Perplexity made 1.1% of the requests (Search Engine Journal on Ahrefs data).

Meanwhile AI search keeps growing as a buying channel: Forrester reports that 94% of business buyers use AI in their buying process. The question is where your effort pays off.

What is llms.txt?

llms.txt is a proposed text file at the root of a website that lists its key pages in Markdown so language models can find them.

It sits next to robots.txt and sitemap.xml. Unlike those, there is no public evidence that AI search crawlers use it to choose sources.

Does llms.txt improve AI citations?

The llms.txt effect is, on current evidence, zero: a 300,000-domain study found no link between having the file and being cited by AI assistants.

Why do so many sites still publish one?

Publishing llms.txt is a low-cost bet: it takes an hour, does no measurable harm, and may matter if AI companies adopt the standard later.

We publish one ourselves, generated automatically by our WordPress theme, because it costs nothing to keep. There is one real audience: coding agents made 10% of requests in the Ahrefs data. If you have developer documentation, a clean llms.txt helps those tools.

What should you do instead?

The better investment is what assistants do read: crawlable HTML, answer-first pages, structured data, fresh facts and mentions on other sites.

  1. Allow the search crawlers: OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Bingbot.
  2. Serve full HTML, so crawlers that do not run JavaScript see your text.
  3. Lead each section with the answer, in two sentences under a question-shaped heading.
  4. Put key facts in text and tables, not in images or PDFs.
  5. Earn mentions on other sites; branded mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at 0.664 in Ahrefs' 2025 study.
  6. Submit your sitemap to Bing, the index behind Microsoft Copilot.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google use llms.txt?

Googlebot made 13% of llms.txt requests in the Ahrefs data, but a request is not use. No study has shown a ranking or citation effect from the file.

Should we remove our llms.txt?

No need. It does no measurable harm. Just do not count it as GEO work.

What about llms-full.txt?

The same applies: no published evidence links it to AI citations.

Could llms.txt matter in the future?

It could, if AI companies adopt it. Keeping a simple, accurate file is cheap insurance.

Who reads llms.txt today?

Mostly SEO audit tools, unidentified bots, Googlebot and coding agents, according to the Ahrefs data. AI search assistants made a small share of requests.
