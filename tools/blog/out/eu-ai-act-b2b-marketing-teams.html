Key takeaways

Article 50 of the EU AI Act has applied since 2 August 2026. For marketing teams that means disclosed chatbots, labelled deepfakes, vendors that mark AI output (2 December 2026 for tools already on the market) and human editorial control. Fines reach €15 million or 3% of turnover.

Marketing is one of the heaviest AI users in most companies, and the first part of the EU AI Act that touches daily marketing work is now live. Article 50, the transparency rule, has applied since 2 August 2026 (European Commission).

The July 2026 Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (White & Case), moved the high-risk rules for Annex III systems to 2 December 2027 (Orrick). It did not move Article 50. Breaches of these obligations can cost up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (Goodwin).

What is Article 50 of the EU AI Act?

Article 50 is the EU AI Act's transparency rule: people must be told when they deal with an AI system or see certain AI-generated content.

It sets four duties, split between providers (who build or sell the AI system) and deployers (the companies that use it). The full text is on the AI Act Service Desk.

  1. Interaction. Providers must design systems that talk with people, such as chatbots, so users know they deal with AI, unless that is obvious.
  2. Marking. Providers of systems that generate audio, images, video or text must mark the output in a machine-readable, detectable way. Assistive editing tools are exempt.
  3. Emotion recognition. Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation must inform the people exposed to it.
  4. Deepfakes and public-interest text. Deployers must disclose deepfakes, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, unless a human reviewed it under editorial responsibility.
  5. Timing. All four apply from 2 August 2026, with one grace period for marking by existing tools.

What did the Digital Omnibus change?

The Digital Omnibus is the July 2026 amendment that delayed most high-risk AI rules but kept Article 50 transparency duties on schedule.

  • It entered into force on 27 July 2026.
  • High-risk systems in Annex III, such as hiring and education tools, now apply from 2 December 2027. Annex I systems in regulated products follow on 2 August 2028.
  • Generative tools already on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to mark their output.
  • The AI literacy duty in Article 4 was softened: organizations must take measures that support AI literacy, and certifying each person is not required (Lewis Silkin).
  • It adds new bans on nudification apps and on systems that generate child sexual abuse material.

Which marketing activities trigger obligations?

A marketing obligation under Article 50 is triggered by four uses: website chatbots, deepfakes of real people, unreviewed public-interest AI text and generative tools.

Does the AI Act require a label on AI-written blog posts?

An AI disclosure on a blog post is required only when it informs the public on matters of public interest and nobody reviewed it editorially.

The Commission's FAQ describes matters of public interest as consumer safety and economic, financial, political, scientific or cultural developments that may be a subject of public debate (European Commission). A typical B2B how-to or product page is usually not that kind of text. Market commentary and research findings can be.

The exemption has two conditions: the text went through human review or editorial control, and a person or company holds editorial responsibility for the publication. In practice, name an editor and keep a short review log. Google expects the same discipline for search, since its guidance says all AI-generated content should be fact-checked and reviewed by a person (Google Search Central).

How should a marketing team prepare?

Preparing a marketing team means mapping every AI tool and use, checking vendor marking, adding disclosures where needed, and assigning a human editor.

  1. List every AI tool the team uses, including AI features inside software you already pay for.
  2. Mark which uses reach customers or the public: chatbots, images, video, voice and published text.
  3. Ask each generative-tool vendor how it marks output and whether it meets the 2 December 2026 deadline.
  4. Add a clear line such as "You are chatting with an AI assistant" to chatbots if the vendor does not show one.
  5. Write a deepfake rule: no synthetic real people, places or events without disclosure.
  6. Send AI-written public content through a named editor and keep a simple review log.
  7. Document the AI training you give the team as evidence of AI literacy measures under Article 4.

Frequently asked questions

Does Article 50 apply to companies outside the EU?

Yes, when their AI output is used in the EU. The AI Act covers providers and deployers in third countries where the output of the AI system is used in the Union.

Do we have to label every AI-generated image?

Not with a visible label in most cases. The provider of the generative tool must mark output in a machine-readable way. Your company must disclose deepfakes: content that resembles real people, places or events and could falsely appear authentic.

What are the fines for breaking Article 50?

Up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. For SMEs and start-ups the lower of the two applies.

When do the high-risk rules apply?

From 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems such as hiring and education tools, and from 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems in regulated products. Typical marketing tools are not on these lists.

Is AI literacy training mandatory?

Article 4 requires providers and deployers to take measures that support AI literacy. Since the Omnibus, certifying each employee is not required. Documenting training and awareness sessions is the practical approach.

Does Article 50 cover AI-written ad copy?

Ad copy is rarely text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, so that disclosure duty usually does not apply. Deepfakes in ads always need a disclosure.
